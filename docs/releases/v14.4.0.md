# Shardelix 14.4.0 — native physical-scan performance and generalization

Pixel-only reconstruction: no OCR, ML, pretrained weights or reference image.
The recorded benchmark samples are development data, except the three
additional pages evaluated after fixing the scaling policy.

## Changes

- Same-page patch nearest-neighbor matching runs with at most four bounded
  native workers, batching independent queries; WebAssembly remains
  single-threaded. Patch coordinates, neighbor count and approximation
  tolerances are unchanged.
- Replace the MILP refinement with exact assignment relaxation and
  subtour-arc branching on the same sparse edge shortlist. Every feasible
  Hamiltonian path must break at least one arc of each disconnected cycle.
  A best-first queue uses the assignment bound, retains the best feasible
  incumbent and stops on time or node budget. The shortlist objective is
  not a proof of real reconstruction accuracy.
- Preserve native horizontal detail on high-resolution equal-width cuts;
  use anti-aliased common x/y scaling for variable-width high-resolution
  cuts to avoid compressing only the vertical stroke geometry. Resizing
  affects matching evidence only, not the rectified output pixels.
- Permit extra width variation only on very elongated, substantially
  filled silhouettes. Interior holes remain guarded.
- Add a benchmark adapter for the author's mechanical strip images and
  foreground masks. See [dataset protocol](../DATASETS.md).

## Paired results on real DocDataset

Balanced, same anonymization seed 624 and shared development machine;
the speed-only change was measured before broadening the scan geometry.
These three scans have at most 1,800 rows, so the new scaling policy does
not modify their matching raster.

| Page | V14.3 (s) | Speed patch (s) | Correct directed joins |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| real1_27 | 14.03 | 8.12 | 26/26 |
| real2_56 | 47.61 | 28.51 | 55/55 |
| real3_39 | 27.78 | 16.42 | 38/38 |
| Total | 89.42 | 53.05 | 119/119 |

1.69x faster (40.7% less elapsed reconstruction time); all 122 positions
correct in both runs. A separate full final-code run, seed 2026, retained
119/119 directed joins and 122/122 positions. Its timings were collected
alongside other development tasks and are not a controlled speed comparison.

## Mechanical document benchmark

The 18 development pages are externally sourced actual mechanical shreds,
not simulated tears. Primary metric is directed adjacency after allowing
one global chain reversal. Rejected pages count as zero, not excluded.

| Collection | Pages | V14.3 | V14.4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D1 / S-MARQUES | 4 | 81/95 | 81/95 |
| D2 / S-ISRI-OCR | 4 | 67/97 | 82/97 |
| D3 / S-CDIP | 10 | 51/225 | 132/225 |
| Total | 18 | 199/417 (47.7%) | 295/417 (70.7%) |

The two previously rejected pages D2/D007 and D3/D095 are now admitted;
they remain incomplete in accuracy. The equal-width scans retain their
old adjacency scores. The new scaling improves performance on varying-width
scans, though D3/D005 regresses by one join. The three additional sampled
pages (not used in deciding the scale policy) improve 20/74 to 26/74 joins
and 6/77 to 14/77 reversal-aligned positions. D2/D010 remains rejected
because of its near-uniform-strip guard.

These measurements cover matching only, not the full Studio workflow.
The 21 selected pages are not a representative random sample of the full
dataset. Matching-only runtime excludes conversion, rendering and export;
the application still enforces its configured canvas and input budgets.
Global reversal-aligned sequence scores do not verify per-piece rotation
or subpixel alignment. The complete dataset was not tested.

## Damage stress and remaining weaknesses

Eight deterministic simulated scan cases, already used in previous
development, retain seven adjacency scores and regress on pageA-32
(12/31 to 8/31). Total undirected joins decline 77/248 to 73/248.
This is not a damage-accuracy improvement claim. Lower visual cost
does not always correspond to correct seams in highly ambiguous documents.
Blank/repeated edges, handwritten content, mixed text/image forms, and
severe asymmetric rim loss remain challenging; a correct strip sequence
also does not establish correct vertical or pixel-level alignment.
